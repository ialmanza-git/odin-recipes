Ramen Recipe

Dish Description

Ramen is a fascinating story of cultural migration, industrialization, and culinary evolution. The Chinese Origins: The roots of ramen lie in Chinese wheat noodle dishes brought to Japanese port cities like Yokohama in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It was originally called Shina Soba (Chinese noodles) and featured a simple, clear broth with sliced pork.

Ingredients

  • Bones
  • Aromatics
  • Kombu & Dried Shiitake
  • Shoyu
  • Miso

  • Chashu
  • Ajitsuke Tamago
  • Scallions
  • Menma

Preparation

  1. Prepare the Components

    • The Broth (The Soul): Soak pork femur bones in cold water for hours to extract any marrow blood, then blanch and scrub them clean. Place them in a massive pot of water and boil violently on high heat for 12 to 18 hours. This continuous, aggressive boiling forces the fat, collagen, and marrow to emulsify into the water, transforming a clear broth into a creamy, opaque white milk.

    • The Tare (The Flavor Base): Ramen broth itself is usually unseasoned. The salt and flavor identity come from the tare placed at the bottom of the bowl. For tonkotsu, a Shio (salt) or Shoyu (soy sauce) tare is made by simmering soy sauce, mirin, sake, kombu (kelp), and dried shiitake mushrooms.

    • The Chashu Pork: Roll a slab of pork belly into a log, tie it with butcher's twine, and sear the outside. Braise it low and slow for hours in a mixture of soy sauce, ginger, garlic, and green onions until it is melt-in-your-mouth tender.

    • The Ajitsuke Tamago (Ramen Egg): Soft-boil eggs for exactly 6 to 6.5 minutes so the whites are set but the yolk remains jammy and liquid. Immediately shock them in ice water, peel them, and marinate them in a soy-mirin mixture overnight.

  2. Assemble the Bowl

    3. Ramen assembly is a fast-paced, precise ritual performed right before serving so the noodles don't get soggy:

    • Warm the Bowl: Ladle hot water into a deep ramen bowl to heat the ceramic, then discard the water.

    • Add Tare and Oil: Pour 1 to 2 tablespoons of your flavor tare into the bottom of the warm bowl, along with a splash of aromatic fat (like black garlic oil or scallion lard).

    • Pour Broth: Ladle the boiling-hot, creamy pork bone broth directly over the tare, whisking gently to combine.

    • Fold the Noodles: Boil fresh, thin, high-alkaline wheat noodles for only about 60 to 90 seconds so they stay katamen (firm). Drain them aggressively to remove excess water, then fold them neatly into the broth.

    • Artful Topping: Arrange your thin slices of chashu pork, a halved marinated egg, a handful of finely sliced green scallions, wood ear mushrooms (kikurage), and a crisp sheet of nori (seaweed) standing against the back of the bowl.

    • Eat Fast: Ramen must be eaten within 5 to 7 minutes of assembly. Slurping the noodles vigorously is highly encouraged—it draws air into your mouth, cooling the boiling broth while simultaneously vaporizing the aromas, intensifying the flavor experience.
