Description:
Lasagna is an iconic Italian baked dish made of stacked layers of wide, 
flat pasta sheets alternating with rich fillings like meat sauce (ragù), 
creamy béchamel or ricotta cheese, and melted mozzarella or Parmigiano-Reggiano.


1. The Pasta

• Lasagna noodles: You can use standard dry lasagna sheets (boiled al dente), 
no-boil lasagna sheets, or fresh pasta sheets.

2. The Meat Sauce

• Meat: Ground beef (or a 50/50 mix of ground beef and sweet Italian sausage).
• Aromatics: Yellow onion and minced garlic.
• Tomatoes: Canned crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, or a high-quality jar of marinara/pasta sauce.
• Seasonings: Dried basil, dried oregano, salt, black pepper, and optionally a pinch of sugar to balance the tomato acidity.

3. The Cheese Filling & Topping

• Ricotta cheese: Provides the traditional creamy, moist layers (Note: Some recipes substitute this with cottage cheese).
• Mozzarella cheese: Shredded or sliced to melt between layers and create a bubbly, golden top crust.
• Parmesan cheese: Finely grated for a sharp, savory bite.
• Egg: One or two large eggs mixed into the ricotta to act as a binder so the lasagna holds its shape when sliced.
• Fresh herbs: Chopped fresh parsley or basil mixed into the cheese filling

Steps:

1. Prepare the Components

• The Meat Sauce (Ragù): Brown minced onions, carrots, celery, 
  and garlic in olive oil. Add ground beef and pork, pouring in a splash of dry white wine to reduce. 
  Stir in tomato purée or crushed tomatoes and let it simmer low and slow for at least 1 to 2 hours 
  until incredibly thick and flavorful.
• The Béchamel (White Sauce): Melt butter in a saucepan, whisk in an equal amount of flour to make a paste (roux), 
and gradually stream in warm milk. Whisk constantly over medium heat until thick and velvety, 
finishing with a pinch of salt and ground nutmeg.
• The Pasta: Boil your lasagna sheets in salted water until just al dente (firm to the bite). 
Drain them and lay them flat on clean towels to dry so they are manageable. 
(Note: If using no-boil sheets, skip this step).

2. Assemble the Layers

Grab a deep, oven-safe baking dish. You will want to build a minimum of three to four layers:
1. Base: Spread a thin layer of meat sauce over the bottom of the pan to stop the pasta from sticking.
2. Pasta: Arrange pasta sheets side-by-side, overlapping slightly to completely cover the sauce.
3. Fillings: Spread a uniform layer of ragù, drizzle with the creamy white béchamel sauce, and sprinkle generously 
with freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano or mozzarella cheese.
4. Repeat: Place another layer of pasta on top and repeat the pattern until your ingredients are almost used up.
5. Top Layer: Finish with a final layer of pasta topped entirely with béchamel,
 a small swirl of meat sauce, and an extra heavy blanket of cheese to achieve a beautiful crust.

3. Bake and Rest

• Bake: Cover the dish loosely with aluminum foil to keep the moisture trapped. 
Bake in a preheated oven at 350°F (180°C) for roughly 20 to 30 minutes. 
Remove the foil for the final 10 minutes to let the cheese brown and become crisp around the edges.
• Rest: Once out of the oven, let the lasagna rest for 15 to 20 minutes before slicing. 
This allows the structural layers to cool slightly and firm up, ensuring clean, beautiful slices 
that don't slide apart on the plate.
